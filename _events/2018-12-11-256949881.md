Ottawa Civic Tech and Speculative Futures Ottawa invite you to a special event for our 98th hacknight session. As technology becomes increasingly accessible and open for citizens to contribute to within their civic environment, how can we build our future cities together? What is the role of creativity and imagination in the context of civic tech and policy design? What are the narratives we are creating for our shared futures as we develop technologies that are for the public good? How will the environment we live in shape new technologies and vice versa?
Join our 3 panellists on December 11th at Impact Hub Ottawa.
------
Remco Volmer, Artengine
Remco is the managing director of Artengine, Ottawa's lab for creative technology. A graduate of Utrecht University of Art’s Creative Media program, he has focused his practice on technologically mediated interaction design. As a cultural producer, he has organized a variety of exhibitions and festivals, including the Electric Fields festival of electronic art and Maker Faire Ottawa. He has a strong interest in innovation in the arts, both on the individual level of the artist and on the combined level of the institution.
------
Natale Dankotuwage, Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada
Born and raised in the Greater Toronto Area, Natale Dankotuwage is an Undergraduate from University of Toronto in Political Science and a Master’s Graduate from Ontario College of Art and Design in Strategic Foresight and Innovation.
As a Social Innovator, she has worked on post-war development with the Asia Foundation. And has worked as a community manager of co-working spaces for digital tech entrepreneurs in Bangalore India and Toronto, Canada. These days she is a Federal Public Servant working for the Government of Canada on digital innovation. Her passion for social innovation persists. She was a Global Ambassador at the 2018 Unleashed Global Innovation Summit in Singapore.
Change-making has been a passion of hers for years. Believing we all have the potential to make a positive difference in our communities, nation and world.
------
Tom Baynes, Policy Horizons Canada
Tom is an intellectual adventurer with a background in anthropology, art history, and public administration, connecting intangible aspects of human experience to concrete social policy issues. He strives to show the value of qualitative research methods in making informed choices about the future, and translate humanistic perspectives into usable insights. At home, his neuroticism and intellectual perversity are seen in his passion for textile crafts, bad television, great movies, and books on aesthetics, social theory, and cultural history.
