You're invited to Ottawa Civic Tech's first full meetup of 2020! It was great to get together socially in January—let's reconnect in a more traditional setting over some talks and demos.
Ottawa Civic Tech "hacknights" are held Tuesday evenings once or twice a month from 6:00-8:00pm. Free and open to all, hacknights bring together people from all walks of life to connect, learn, share, and collaborate.
You do not need to be a "techie" to attend - everyone is welcome to participate and contribute to building civic good through technology, data, design, policy, advocacy & citizen engagement. Join us!

Pia Andrews is an open government and data ninja who has been working in making the world a better place for 20 years. She currently works within the (public sector) machine to transform public services through greater transparency, democratic engagement, citizen-centric design, open data, emerging technologies and real, pragmatic actual innovation in the public sector and beyond. She believes that tech culture has a huge role to play in achieving better policy planning, outcomes, public engagement and a better public service all round. She is also trying to do her part in establishing greater public benefit from publicly funded data, software and research. Pia was recognised in 2018 and 2019 as one of the global top 20 most Influential in Digital Government and was awarded as one of the Top 100 Most Influential Women in Australia for 2014.
Pia is currently the Digital Lead and Special Advisor for the Benefits Delivery Modernization program at Service Canada (ESDC) in Ottawa, Canada. There she is helping design and deliver a holistic and modern digital channel for the Canadian Government to deliver a delightful, dignified, user-centric, responsive and integrated digital journey that motivates and empowers people to help themselves.
Full bio: http://pipka.org/standard-bio/

Mark Richardson, HousingNowTO.com: The City of Toronto has set an ambitious target for “creating 40,000 new units of Affordable-Housing by 2031” – but the City never met any of the previous “Affordable-Housing” targets it has set in the 20+ year since amalgamation. How can citizens, technologists and housing-advocates use public-data, civic-tech tools and innovation to change the usual neighbourhood-conversations about new housing developments near existing transit? Since it was launched in January 2019, the HousingNowTO.com public-mapping and social-media project has tried to answer those questions for the 38,000+ visitors to our free, public information site.
